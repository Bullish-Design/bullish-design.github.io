Molded plywood and the Eames DCW chair

The 1946 Dining Chair Wood (DCW) by Charles and Ray Eames connects material, structure, and comfort. The Museum of Modern Art records the chair as molded and bent birch plywood with rubber shockmounts.

The Eameses had experimented with plywood since 1940. The Museum of Modern Art describes how their early work did not yet produce complex curves that could support the body without upholstery. Years of experiments led to a process that could bend and mold the material into compound curves.

Herman Miller’s design history describes a limit in the original single-shell idea. The shell did not withstand stress where the seat and back met. The designers moved to separate molded panels for the seat and back.

A design reading

The case shows how material and process can shape a product’s structure and appearance. The split between seat and back answered a documented stress problem. It also became part of the chair’s visual form. This reading connects the sources; it is not a direct historical quote.
